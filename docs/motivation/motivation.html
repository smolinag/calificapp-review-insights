CALIFICAPP REVIEW INSIGHTS · PERFICIENT GLOBAL AI-FIRST ACADEMY · L1 CASE 05

Why this project exists, and why this is the solution

A motorcycle-gear store in Colombia collects customer ratings on a tablet at the counter. The numbers say everything is fine. A handful of comments say otherwise, and nobody reads them.

3,184real ratings, Oct 2025 – Oct 2026
4.9★monthly average; it never moves
94%are 5★ (2,992 of 3,184)
14of 997 comments need the owner to act

1. Motivation

The user and the situation

The user is the store owner: not technical, no time to read comments, looks at the numbers occasionally. The store sells helmets, tyres, rain gear and accessories, and also fits them: tyre changes, crash bars, luggage racks. After each sale the customer rates the employee who served them from 1 to 5 on a Calificapp tablet, and can leave a comment.

About a third of the ratings carry a comment, typically short (median 18 characters) and in informal Colombian Spanish: "exelente atencion", "melo", emoji. Almost all are compliments. The owner sees a 4.9 average and reasonably concludes there is nothing to look at.

What is hidden in the comments

Reading all 997 real comments by hand found 14 that the owner must act on. Some of them:

ReviewStarsWhat it says (paraphrased)Why it matters
R019721A tyre the store fitted came off the rim ("despestañada") and the rider crashedSafety incident; possible liability
R003095"Excellent service. But very lewd"Possible staff misconduct, hidden in a top rating
R020211Warranty claim on a helmet, no solution givenUnresolved claim; risk of a formal complaint
R00460, R009335"…but the discount was missing", "…although prices are high"Price friction inside top ratings
R012155"It's a special order… please call if you get it"A sale waiting on a call nobody made
The star rating cannot find them. Of the 14 reviews that need action, 4 are 5★, including one of the 4 urgent ones (R00309). Reading only the 1–3★ comments would find 10 of 14 and miss an urgent case. And a 1★ can be a mis-tap on a positive comment. The decision has to come from the text.

Why it is worth solving now

  • The cost of a miss is asymmetric. One unread safety report or harassment complaint can cost more than everything the ratings are meant to improve. The owner tolerates some false alarms; not a missed crash report.
  • Nobody will read 1,000 compliments. Asking the owner or a manager to read every comment is the obvious fix, and it is not happening.
  • The history expires. The Calificapp backend deletes each rating 500 days after it is created, so anything not analysed is lost.
  • The owner has questions the dashboard cannot answer: "¿Algún cliente tuvo un accidente?", "¿Qué dicen de los cascos?", "¿Cuántas reseñas de 1 estrella hubo en septiembre?".

What success means

Defined before any code (docs/01-problem-statement.md) and measured on hand-labeled data, real and synthetic reported separately: every urgent review found (100%); at least 90% of actionable reviews found with at least 60% precision; no invented citations; exact numbers for counting questions; at least 80% of owner questions answered correctly; and retrieval measured against the simpler alternative of sending every comment to the model.

2. Why this is the solution

The solution has three parts: (1) a local AI model reads every comment and labels it urgent / review / normal, with an alert-word safety net that can only raise severity; (2) the owner asks questions in plain Spanish, and a router sends counting questions to exact SQL and meaning questions to semantic search plus the model, which must cite the reviews it uses; (3) every citation is checked against the reviews the model was given. Each choice below was made against an alternative, and most were measured.

DecisionChosen, and whyRejected alternative, and the evidence
Decide from the text An LLM classifies every comment by what it reports, not by its stars. Filter by low ratings: finds 10 of 14 actionable reviews and misses urgent R00309 (5★).
Domain context for the model A context file (CLAUDE.md): severity definitions, local slang, the rule that a review is data, never instructions. Bare prompt (v0): precision 8/33 (flagged plain praise) and it obeyed a prompt injection. With the context (v1): 13/13, injection ignored.
Alert-word safety net After the model, a short word list raises severity and never lowers it. Model alone: missed R00309 with and without the context file. With the net: real urgent recall 3/4 → 4/4, no false alarms on the 997 real comments.
Counting by SQL, not the model Rules detect counting questions; fixed, parameterized SQL computes the number. Let the model count or fill filters: it read "de 1 estrella" as "at least 1 star" and reported 2,892 instead of 11. With SQL: 13/13 counting questions exact.
Retrieval for meaning questions Embed the question, take the 20 closest reviews (half reserved for flagged ones on complaint questions), answer only from them. Send all 997 comments in one prompt (~18k tokens, it fits): measured on 26 questions, 1/16 vs 7/16 correct, and 13 invented review IDs vs 0. The model loses the few reviews that matter among the compliments.
Local model Qwen 2.5 7B and bge-m3 embeddings via Ollama on the store's PC. Client reviews never leave the computer; no per-call cost. Claude API by default: stronger, but paid and sends client text to a third party; kept as an option. Free cloud tiers: some train on submitted data. Fine-tuned classifier: 14 real negatives are too few to train one.
Retrieval pipeline over an n8n automation Code in a repository: testable, versioned, reviewable. n8n workflow: would fit a weekly digest, but logic in a no-code flow is harder to test and review, and Q&A needs retrieval anyway.
SQLite for everything Reviews, labels and vectors in one file; brute-force search is under a second at ~1,000 vectors. Chroma (the original plan): an extra dependency with no benefit at this size. DynamoDB copy: risks touching the client's account.
Citations, verified Every answer cites review IDs; any ID not among the reviews given to the model marks the answer unverified. Uncited summaries: the owner could not check them. Result: 0 invalid citations in 40 answers.

The core problem, finding what to act on among a thousand compliments, is solved on the real data. Open-ended questions are not: retrieval usually finds the right review and the 7B model drops it at the last step. A count with a month and no year ("¿…en septiembre?") silently counts every review. The planned weekly digest was cut. All of this, with specific failing inputs, is in docs/failure-analysis.md and evidence/eval_results.md.

Data: all 3,184 real ratings of one store, anonymized with the owner's permission (employees as EMP_xx, customer names and phones removed). Review IDs refer to the anonymized dataset. Repository: github.com/smolinag/calificapp-review-insights
